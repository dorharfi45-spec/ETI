# Geometry Base — High School Reference

## 1. Angles formed by parallel lines and a transversal

When two **parallel lines** are cut by a transversal, eight angles are formed in two groups of four. Key relationships:

- **Corresponding angles** — same position at each intersection (e.g. both top-left). They are **equal**.
- **Alternate interior angles** — on opposite sides of the transversal, between the two lines. They are **equal**.
- **Alternate exterior angles** — on opposite sides of the transversal, outside the two lines. They are **equal**.
- **Co-interior angles** (also called *consecutive interior* or *same-side interior* angles) — on the same side of the transversal, between the lines. They are **supplementary**: `a + b = 180°`.
- **Vertical angles** (formed at any single intersection) are always **equal**, whether or not lines are parallel.

**Converse:** if corresponding angles are equal (or alternate angles are equal, or co-interior angles are supplementary), then the two lines *are* parallel. These converses are the standard way to *prove* two lines are parallel.

## 2. Triangles — rules that apply to every triangle

- **Angle sum theorem:** the three interior angles of any triangle sum to **180°**.
- **Exterior angle theorem:** an exterior angle of a triangle equals the **sum of the two remote (non-adjacent) interior angles**. Also, each exterior angle is supplementary to its adjacent interior angle.
- **Sum of exterior angles:** the three exterior angles (one per vertex) sum to **360°**.

- **Triangle inequality:** the sum of any two sides must be **greater than** the third side (`a + b > c`), and the difference of any two sides is **less than** the third side.
- **Side–angle relationship:** the largest side is opposite the largest angle; the smallest side is opposite the smallest angle. Equal sides are opposite equal angles.
- **Midsegment theorem:** a segment joining the midpoints of two sides is **parallel** to the third side and **half its length**.
- **Sum of medians / centroid:** the three medians meet at the **centroid**, which divides each median in a **2:1** ratio (2 parts toward the vertex).
- **Altitudes:** the three altitudes meet at the **orthocenter**.
- **Perpendicular bisectors:** the three perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet at the **circumcenter** (equidistant from all three vertices — center of the circumscribed circle).
- **Angle bisectors:** the three angle bisectors meet at the **incenter** (equidistant from all three sides — center of the inscribed circle).
- **Area formulas:**
  - `Area = ½ · base · height`
  - `Area = ½ · a · b · sin(C)` (two sides and the included angle)
  - `Area = (a² · sin B · sin C) / (2 sin A)` (one side and its two adjacent angles)
  - **Heron's formula** (three sides only, no height/angle needed): `Area = √(s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c))`, where `s = (a+b+c)/2` is the semi-perimeter.
  - *(The two sine-based formulas above come straight from the Law of Sines/Cosines — see §7.2, Trigonometric geometry.)*

### Triangle Congruence Theorems
Two triangles are **congruent** (identical in shape and size) if one of these holds:

- **SSS** — all three sides equal.
- **SAS** — two sides and the **included** angle equal.
- **ASA** — two angles and the **included** side equal.
- **AAS** — two angles and a **non-included** side equal.
- **HL (Hypotenuse–Leg)** — right triangles only: hypotenuse and one leg equal.

*(Note: SSA is generally **not** a valid congruence test — it can produce two different triangles, the "ambiguous case.")*

### Triangle Similarity Theorems
Two triangles are **similar** (same shape, proportional sides) if one of these holds:

- **AA (Angle-Angle)** — two angles equal (the third is automatically equal, since angles sum to 180°).
- **SSS~** — all three pairs of corresponding sides are in the **same ratio**.
- **SAS~** — two pairs of sides in the same ratio, with the **included** angle equal.

In similar triangles: corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional, and the ratio of areas equals the **square** of the ratio of sides.

### Attributes of specific triangle types

- **Equilateral triangle:** all three sides equal, all three angles equal to **60°**. Every equilateral triangle is also isosceles. The median, altitude, and angle bisector from any vertex coincide (same segment). **Area** `= (√3/4) · side²`.
- **Isosceles triangle:** two sides equal (the "legs"), and the angles opposite them (the **base angles**) are equal. The altitude/median/angle bisector drawn from the apex (the vertex between the two equal sides) to the base coincide, and this segment is also the perpendicular bisector of the base.
- **Scalene triangle:** all sides and all angles are different lengths/measures — no special symmetry.
- **Right triangle:** one angle is 90°. Key facts:
  - **Pythagorean theorem:** `a² + b² = c²`, where `c` is the hypotenuse.
  - **Converse of the Pythagorean theorem:** if `a² + b² = c²`, the triangle is right-angled.
  - The altitude drawn from the right angle to the hypotenuse creates two smaller triangles similar to the original and to each other, giving: `h² = p · q` (the altitude is the geometric mean of the two hypotenuse segments), and each leg is the geometric mean of the hypotenuse and the adjacent segment.
  - **Special right triangles:** 45°-45°-90° → side ratio `1 : 1 : √2`. 30°-60°-90° → side ratio `1 : √3 : 2`.
  - The **median to the hypotenuse** equals half the hypotenuse (and equals the circumradius).
- **Obtuse triangle:** one angle greater than 90°. **Acute triangle:** all three angles less than 90°.

## 3. Quadrilaterals

- **Sum of interior angles of any quadrilateral: 360°** (derived from splitting it into two triangles).

### Attributes of specific quadrilateral types

- **Parallelogram:** both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
  - Opposite sides are **equal**.
  - Opposite angles are **equal**.
  - Consecutive (adjacent) angles are **supplementary** (sum to 180°).
  - Diagonals **bisect each other**.
- **Rectangle** (a parallelogram with a right angle):
  - All the parallelogram properties, **plus**: all four angles are **90°**.
  - Diagonals are **equal in length** (as well as bisecting each other).
- **Rhombus** (a parallelogram with two adjacent equal sides):
  - All the parallelogram properties, **plus**: all four sides are **equal**.
  - Diagonals are **perpendicular** to each other, and each diagonal **bisects** a pair of opposite angles.
- **Square:** a rectangle **and** a rhombus — all sides equal, all angles 90°, diagonals equal, perpendicular, and bisect each other and the vertex angles (each diagonal splits a corner into two 45° angles).
- **Trapezoid (trapezium):** exactly one pair of opposite sides parallel (the "bases"). The other two sides are the "legs."
  - **Midsegment (median) of a trapezoid:** the segment joining the midpoints of the legs is parallel to both bases and equal to their **average**: `midsegment = (base₁ + base₂) / 2`.
  - **Isosceles trapezoid** (legs equal): base angles are equal (in each pair), diagonals are equal in length.
- **Kite:** two distinct pairs of **adjacent** sides equal (not opposite, as in a rhombus).
  - Diagonals are **perpendicular**.
  - One diagonal (the one between the vertices where the equal sides meet) **bisects** the other diagonal and bisects the two angles at its endpoints.

### Area formulas

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Parallelogram | `base × height`  =  `a·b·sin(θ)` (two sides & included angle) |
| Rectangle | `length × width` |
| Rhombus | `(d₁ × d₂) / 2` (half the product of the diagonals)  =  `base × height` |
| Square | `side²`  =  `diagonal² / 2` |
| Trapezoid | `((base₁ + base₂) / 2) × height`  =  `midsegment × height` |
| Kite | `(d₁ × d₂) / 2` (half the product of the diagonals) |

## 4. Polygons — angle sum formulas

For any **convex polygon** with `n` sides:

- **Sum of interior angles:** `(n − 2) · 180°`
- **Each interior angle of a *regular* polygon:** `(n − 2) · 180° / n`
- **Sum of exterior angles:** always **360°**, regardless of the number of sides (for any convex polygon).
- **Each exterior angle of a *regular* polygon:** `360° / n`
- **Number of diagonals:** `n(n − 3) / 2`

## 5. Thales' Theorem and its variations

- **Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales' Theorem in a triangle):** if a line is drawn **parallel** to one side of a triangle, intersecting the other two sides, it divides those two sides **proportionally**.
  If `DE ∥ BC` in triangle `ABC` (D on AB, E on AC), then: `AD/DB = AE/EC` (equivalently `AD/AB = AE/AC`).

- **Intercept Theorem (generalized Thales' Theorem):** if three or more **parallel lines** cut two transversals, they divide the transversals into **proportional segments**. - **Thales' Theorem in a circle:** if `A`, `B`, `C` are points on a circle where `AC` is a **diameter**, then angle `ABC` is a **right angle (90°)** — i.e., **any angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle**.

## 6. Circles — chords, angles, and tangents

### Chords
- A **radius (or diameter) perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord** (and the arc it cuts off) — and conversely, the perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the center.
- **Equal chords** are **equidistant from the center**; conversely, chords equidistant from the center are equal.
- **Longer chords** are closer to the center than shorter chords.
- **Intersecting Chords Theorem:** if two chords `AB` and `CD` intersect inside a circle at point `P`, then `AP · PB = CP · PD`.

### Angles in a circle
- **Central angle:** an angle whose vertex is the center; it equals the measure of its intercepted **arc**.
- **Inscribed Angle Theorem:** an inscribed angle (vertex on the circle) is **half** the central angle that subtends the same arc: `inscribed angle = ½ · central angle` (equivalently, `= ½ · arc`).
- **Angles inscribed in the same arc/segment are equal** — any inscribed angle subtending the same arc has the same measure.
- **Angle in a semicircle = 90°** (special case of the inscribed angle theorem / Thales' theorem — the arc is a semicircle, i.e. 180°, so the inscribed angle is 90°).
- **Cyclic quadrilateral:** a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle. **Opposite angles are supplementary** (sum to 180°). Also, an exterior angle at one vertex equals the interior angle at the opposite vertex.

### Tangents
- A **tangent** to a circle is **perpendicular to the radius** drawn to the point of tangency.
- **Two tangents drawn from the same external point** to a circle are **equal in length**, and the line from the external point to the center **bisects the angle** between the two tangents.
- **Tangent–Chord Angle:** the angle between a tangent and a chord drawn from the point of tangency equals **half the intercepted arc** (equivalently, it equals the inscribed angle on the other side subtending the same chord) — this is the "tangent-chord angle theorem."

### Power of a point (secants and tangents)
- **Two secants** from an external point `P`, meeting the circle at `A,B` and `C,D`: `PA · PB = PC · PD`.
- **Tangent–Secant:** if `PT` is tangent and `PAB` is a secant from the same external point: `PT² = PA · PB`.

### 7.1 Basic definitions — sin, cos, tan (right triangle)

For an acute angle `θ` in a right triangle: **SOH-CAH-TOA**.

- `sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse`
- `cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse`
- `tan θ = opposite / adjacent = sin θ / cos θ`

### 7.2 Sine theorem (Law of Sines), Cosine theorem (Law of Cosines), and area formulas

For a triangle with sides `a, b, c` opposite angles `A, B, C`:

- **Law of Sines:** `a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R` (R = circumradius)
- **Law of Cosines:** `c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos C` (and the same pattern cyclically for `a²`, `b²`)
  - Rearranged to find an angle: `cos C = (a² + b² − c²) / (2ab)`
- **Area using only sines and sides:**
  - `Area = ½ · a · b · sin C` (two sides and the included angle) — same formula listed under §2's triangle area formulas.
  - `Area = (a² · sin B · sin C) / (2 sin A)` (one side and its two adjacent angles — obtained by combining the Law of Sines with the formula above; handy for the AAS/ASA case).

### 7.3 What to look for when choosing which tool to use

- **Three sides known (SSS):** use the **Law of Cosines** to find any angle.
- **Two sides + the included angle (SAS):** use the **Law of Cosines** to find the third side, then the Law of Sines (or Cosines again) for the remaining angles.
- **Two angles + any side (AAS or ASA):** use the **Law of Sines** directly (the third angle follows from `180°` minus the other two).
- **Two sides + a non-included angle (SSA):** use the **Law of Sines**, but watch for the **ambiguous case** — depending on the given lengths this can produce **0, 1, or 2** valid triangles, so check each candidate solution makes sense (angles must sum to `180°`, all sides positive).
- **It's a right triangle:** plain `sin/cos/tan` (SOH-CAH-TOA) is simpler than either theorem — reach for the Law of Sines/Cosines only when there's no right angle to exploit.
